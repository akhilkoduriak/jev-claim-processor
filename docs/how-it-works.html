How the claim processor works

What happens to an insurance claim, from the moment it is submitted to the moment a decision is made
1

A claim comes in

A staff member enters the claim: who the patient is, what treatment they had, how much it cost, and which provider billed it.

For testing, a whole batch of example claims can be loaded at once.

2

An AI model checks it

The claim is sent to Jev, an AI model from TypeSafe AI. It doesn't write text; it answers a fixed set of questions:

Questions it answers include
  • Approve, deny, or send to a person?
  • How sure is that answer?
  • How urgently does someone need to look?
Backup if the AI can't be reached Built-in rules make the call instead: how medically necessary the treatment is, whether the price is unusual, and whether the billing looks odd.
3

A decision is made

ApproveRecommended next step: pay the claim.
DenyRecommended next step: send a denial notice explaining how to appeal.
Send for reviewAnything unusual, and every claim of $50,000 or more, goes to a claims adjuster: a person who makes the final call.
4

It's recorded and reported

Every claim and its decision is saved. The team can see:

  • ✓Totals: how many claims were approved, denied or sent for review
  • ✓How much money was claimed and approved
  • ✓For each claim, the decision and the reasons behind it
Routine claims move quicklyStraightforward claims are decided without waiting in a queue.
People handle the hard casesUnusual prices, odd billing and very large claims are routed to a person.
Every decision is explainedEach outcome comes with a confidence level and the reasons for it.
It always gives an answerIf the AI service is down, the backup rules keep claims moving.
This is a demonstration built with made-up claims. It recommends next steps but does not make payments or send letters.
